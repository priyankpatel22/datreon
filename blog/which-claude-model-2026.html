Which Claude Model Should You Use? A Practical Guide to the 2026 Lineup

Datreon · October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Anthropic no longer ships “Claude” as one model. It ships a lineup — and the price gap between the cheapest and the most capable option is 10×. Choosing well is one of the simplest ways to make an AI project faster, cheaper, and better at the same time.

As of October 2026, the current generation spans four generally available models — Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 — plus Mythos 5.1, which is offered only through a restricted access program. They share a family resemblance, but they're built for very different jobs.

Model by model

Haiku 4.5 — speed and volume

Haiku is the fastest and least expensive model in the lineup. It's the right default for work that is high-volume, well-defined and latency-sensitive: classifying and routing requests, extracting fields from documents, tagging and moderating content, and powering real-time chat where every second counts. It also makes an excellent “worker” — the sub-agent that handles narrow tasks inside a larger system. Its 200K-token context window is the smallest in the lineup, so very large document sets belong elsewhere.

Sonnet 5.5 — the everyday workhorse

The newest Sonnet is the model most teams should evaluate first. It balances capability, speed and cost for everyday software engineering, agentic workflows, customer-facing assistants and knowledge work, and it reads up to a million tokens of context. If you're unsure where to start, start here — then move up or down based on measured results.

Opus 5.5 — when the work is genuinely hard

Opus 5.5 is the strongest model in the Opus line and the default in Anthropic's developer documentation — at a lower per-token price than the Opus it replaced. Reach for it on complex, multi-step coding, large refactors, deep analysis across long documents, and agents that must plan and recover from their own mistakes. A fast mode, in research preview, runs it up to 2.5× faster at premium pricing for latency-sensitive work.

Fable 5.1 — the frontier tier

Fable 5.1 is Anthropic's most capable widely released model, aimed at the most demanding reasoning and long-horizon autonomous work. It always thinks before it answers, and a single hard request can run for many minutes — so it suits research, complex investigations and autonomous projects more than quick chat. Two practical notes for buyers: it costs 2.5× Opus 5.5 per token, and it requires 30-day data retention, so it isn't available under zero-data-retention arrangements unless Anthropic expressly authorizes it. Regulated teams should check that before designing around it.

Mythos 5.1 — restricted

Mythos 5.1 shares Fable's capabilities but is offered only to organizations in a restricted access program. For most teams it isn't an option today; Fable 5.1 is the closest generally available equivalent.

The smartest setup is usually more than one model

The highest-leverage pattern we see is routing: let a fast, inexpensive model triage every request, and escalate only the work that needs more horsepower. Most traffic in a real application is routine — so most of it never has to touch the most expensive model.

How to choose: a practical checklist

  • Start with the cheapest model that might clear your quality bar, and measure it on your real tasks — not on public benchmarks.
  • Judge cost per completed task, not per request. A cheaper model that needs retries or extra turns can end up costing more.
  • Tune effort before you switch models. Current Claude models let you dial how hard they think, and Anthropic's guidance notes that newer models at lower effort often match or beat previous-generation models running at high effort.
  • Cache what repeats. Prompt caching makes large, stable instructions and reference documents far cheaper to resend.
  • Re-test on every release. New Opus and Sonnet models both landed in September 2026 alone. Yesterday's best choice may not be today's.
The right model isn't the most powerful one — it's the least powerful one that reliably does the job.

Model choice compounds. A good routing decision, made once, saves money and latency on every request your system will ever handle — which makes an afternoon of measurement before you commit one of the best investments in an AI project.
